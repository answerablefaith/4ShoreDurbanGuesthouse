Boxer Super Run Durban 2026: race-weekend guide from the Bluff

Published 16 October 2026 · Updated 16 October 2026 · 4Shore Guesthouse

Running or cheering at the Boxer Super Run on Sunday 18 October? The key to a good morning is sorting your race pack, wave and route before the beachfront fills up.

Quick answer: The 2026 Boxer Super Run takes place in Durban on Sunday 18 October. The 5km starts on Snell Parade near Blue Lagoon Park and finishes at Blue Lagoon Park. The open wave begins at 07:00, social waves follow from 08:00, and the elite races start later. Check your confirmed entry and wave before leaving the Bluff.

Know the start and finish before race day

The start is on Snell Parade near Blue Lagoon, while the finish is at Blue Lagoon Park. That difference matters if someone is dropping you off or coming to watch. Agree on a place to meet after the race, away from the busy finish area if possible.

Our view: agree on a landmark away from the busy finish area, rather than relying on calls or a strict timetable. Runners and supporters may arrive at different times. If you're watching with children, make sure each child knows which adult to stay with.

Check which wave is yours

Boxer's event notice lists the open 5km wave at 07:00. Super Social waves run from 08:00 to 09:30. Elite women start at 09:45, elite men at 10:00, and the 1km kids' race at 10:30. Those later starts do not mean every runner can arrive late.

Check the wave on your own entry details. The organiser's wave list says elite places are by invitation, while the open and social waves cater for different runners. If you're watching, decide whose race matters most.

A 07:00 runner needs a different morning from someone watching the elite men's 10:00 start. We'd plan backwards from the actual wave, not from the event's first start. Allow time for the final walk and any checks at the venue. If two people in your group have different waves, decide whether to travel together or meet there. Either can work; waiting around for hours isn't compulsory.

Collect your race pack in advance

Durban Tourism lists race-pack collection at Blue Lagoon Park on 16 October from 09:00 to 17:00 and on 17 October from 09:00 to 14:00. Check the organiser's latest instructions for what to bring and whether your pack is ready.

Collect your pack before Sunday. Confirm what entry details or ID to bring and whether someone else may collect for you.

Saturday collection is worth considering if you're already in Durban. It removes one job from Sunday morning and lets you check that your number and entry details are correct. But don't mistake a Saturday drive for a Sunday route rehearsal. The road closures may change how you reach Blue Lagoon Park on race day.

Expect closures along the beachfront

KZN Athletics reported temporary closures and lane restrictions for 18 October. They affect Snell Parade, parts of Battery Beach Road and the southbound side of Masabalala Yengwa Avenue near Moses Mabhida Stadium.

Preparations start from 03:00 and the course is expected to be clear by 12:30. That is a target, not a promised reopening time. The KZN Athletics route notice also describes a turn onto the promenade near Moses Mabhida Stadium. Follow live road notices and Metro Police directions.

Build the trip around access, not distance

Driving from the Bluff or using a booked ride may both work, but the closest road to the start could be restricted. Check an allowed drop-off or parking area before you set off. Do not tell a driver to use a closed street.

Save the guesthouse address for your return and charge your phone. Our guide to getting around Durban from the Bluff explains transport options.

Here's the trade-off we'd make: accept a longer walk from a permitted drop-off rather than spend time circling closed roads. It may feel less convenient when looking at a map, but it gives the driver a clearer route and leaves the runner with a more predictable final stretch. Anyone with limited mobility should check accessible arrangements with the organiser rather than assume that approach will suit them.

Pack for the run, not for a full beach day

Keep your race number and timing chip with your running kit. Wear the shoes you plan to use, bring what you need for sun protection and check the event rules on bags and personal items.

Supporters need water, a hat and a meeting plan. The organiser lists a minimum age of nine for the 5km. The 1km kids' race has separate entry rules; check before promising a child a place.

Lay out your number, shoes and clothing on Saturday. If you're staying on the Bluff, keep everything in one place so an early departure doesn't become a search for missing kit. We'd also check where the timing chip belongs rather than guessing on the morning.

Make room for the morning after

Once the race is over, you may want breakfast, a break or a return to the Bluff. Keep the next activity open until everyone is finished. Some will want to stay for the event atmosphere; others will be ready to leave.

Make the race the main outing and choose breakfast or a return trip once everyone is together. For a slower afternoon, see our guide to things to do near the Bluff.

We'd leave the rest of Sunday open. The finish area could be busy, and not everyone will feel ready to head straight to another booking. A late breakfast or an afternoon near the Bluff coast may suit the group better. The race is already the main event; you don't need to prove the trip was worthwhile by filling every hour.

For supporters: pick one place to watch

Watching a 5km race can be harder to organise than it sounds. Runners move quickly, streets may be restricted, and a supporter trying to follow someone around the course can end up missing them altogether. Our suggestion is to choose one permitted viewing point, agree on the runner's clothing or number, and arrange a separate meeting place for afterwards. Check official spectator guidance before settling on a spot.

There's a useful difference between being close to the action and being easy to find. We think the second matters more when families or groups are involved. A recognisable landmark outside the busiest finish area is often a better meeting point than 'somewhere near the line'.

What to do the evening before

Pack your running kit and save your entry details where you can read them offline. Agree on the drop-off and meeting point with anyone travelling with you. Check whether the road restrictions change your usual route.

Supporters can arrive for the wave they want to watch rather than turning up with the first runners. Runners need to allow for their start and check-in; spectators need to allow for road access and a clear viewing point. Those are different plans, even when everyone is going to the same event.

FAQ: Boxer Super Run from the Bluff

When is the 2026 Durban Boxer Super Run?

Boxer Super Run Durban is scheduled for Sunday 18 October 2026. Check the organiser's website for changes.

Where should I go for the 5km start?

The organiser lists Snell Parade near Blue Lagoon Park as the start and Blue Lagoon Park as the finish. Plan your drop-off and meeting points around road access.

Can I collect my race pack on Saturday?

Durban Tourism lists Boxer Super Run pack collection at Blue Lagoon Park on Saturday 17 October from 09:00 to 14:00. Confirm collection rules first.

Will roads be closed near the route?

Yes. Boxer Super Run will affect Snell Parade, Battery Beach Road and Masabalala Yengwa Avenue on 18 October. KZN Athletics says preparations start at 03:00.
