Trying bunny chow in Durban: a first-timer guide

Published 31 August 2026 · Updated 31 August 2026 · 4Shore Guesthouse

Bunny chow is one of Durban's best-known foods. If you have never tried it, a few simple choices can make your first order easier and turn the meal into a useful part of your Durban stay rather than just another item on a checklist.

Quick answer: Bunny chow is curry served inside a hollowed piece of white bread. Start with a quarter loaf if you want a manageable first taste, choose a filling you know you enjoy, ask about chilli heat, and check current opening times before you travel.

Know what you are ordering

A bunny chow is not a burger. It is a Durban dish built around bread and curry. The centre of a loaf is removed, the bread is filled with curry, and the piece taken from the middle is usually placed back on top.

National Geographic Traveller highlighted bunny chow in May 2026 as a dish that helped put Durban on the food map. It also noted the long link between the dish and Durban's Indian community.

Common choices include mutton, chicken and beans. Menus differ, so read the options at the place you choose rather than expecting every shop to serve the same fillings.

Our view is that first-timers should treat the bread and curry as one dish, not two separate parts. The bread is not just a container. It soaks up the sauce, changes texture as you eat and is part of what makes the meal distinct from simply ordering curry with bread on the side.

Start with the size

Bunny chow is often sold by loaf size. A quarter loaf is a useful starting point for many first-time visitors. It gives you the full bread-and-curry experience without committing to a much larger meal.

If you are very hungry, ask about the portion before moving up a size. Curry, bread and potatoes can make the meal more filling than it first looks.

We think size is the most useful first decision because it sets the tone for everything else. A first bunny chow is easier to enjoy when you are curious rather than trying to defeat the plate. You can always order more food another time; you cannot make an oversized first meal feel lighter halfway through.

Ask about the heat

Durban curry is known for bold spice and can carry real chilli heat. That does not mean every bunny tastes the same. Recipes vary between cooks and restaurants.

If you do not eat hot food often, ask how spicy your chosen filling is before ordering. Picking a milder option is better than ordering for pride and enjoying none of it.

Heat also changes how much of the rest of the dish you notice. When the chilli level suits you, it is easier to taste the curry itself, the bread, the potatoes and any side accompaniments.

Our opinion is that asking about chilli is not being cautious; it is being practical. The goal is to understand the dish, not prove anything. A good first experience should leave you wanting to try another version later.

Use a simple size-heat-filling rule

If you want a quick way to order, decide in this order:

  • Size: choose a portion that matches your appetite.
  • Heat: ask how spicy the curry is if you are unsure.
  • Filling: pick the meat or bean option you are most likely to enjoy.

This order works because the first two decisions control how manageable the meal feels. The filling then becomes a taste choice rather than another thing to overthink.

Make it one part of the day

For guests staying on the Bluff, treat a bunny chow stop as a food outing rather than another task to squeeze between several attractions. Choose where you want to eat, check the current opening hours, and then plan transport.

Our simple rule is food first, route second, extras third. Decide on the meal before adding a market, city stop or other activity. That keeps the day flexible if lunch takes longer than expected.

This is especially useful in September 2026. Durban Tourism is promoting the Durban Street Food Festival from 4 to 6 September, with local and international street food in the mix. That is a current sign of interest in Durban's food culture, but you do not need a festival ticket to make a local food stop part of your stay.

We think this is a better way to experience local food than building an overpacked “food crawl”. One proper meal gives you time to notice what you are eating, talk about it and decide what you would try differently next time.

Do not chase a claim about the “best”

Search results are full of rankings. A first visit does not need to become a hunt for one perfect bunny chow. Current opening hours, the filling you want, a sensible route and a place that suits your group matter more.

Restaurant details can change. Check the venue directly on the day you plan to go. If you are using a ride service, confirm the destination before you leave.

There is also value in avoiding false precision. One person's favourite may be another person's too hot, too rich or too large. The better question is not “Which bunny chow is objectively best?” but “Which place gives me the kind of first experience I actually want?”

What to expect when it arrives

A bunny chow is usually a hands-on meal. The bread softens as the curry settles, and the top piece can be used to scoop or dip before you move into the loaf itself.

Do not worry about making it look neat. The point is the combination of bread and curry. Keep napkins nearby and take your time, especially if the portion is large or the curry is hot.

If you are sharing, decide that before ordering rather than after the food arrives. One quarter loaf each can be simpler than trying to divide a larger bunny evenly.

FAQ: trying bunny chow in Durban

What is bunny chow?

It is curry served inside a hollowed piece of white bread. It is closely associated with Durban.

What size should I try first?

A quarter loaf is a sensible starting point for many visitors. Check the portion where you order because sizes and serving styles vary.

Is bunny chow always very hot?

No single heat level applies to every recipe. Durban curry can be hot, so ask before ordering if you prefer less chilli.

Should I book a restaurant?

That depends on the venue. Check current opening times and booking rules directly before you leave.
